# Total Cost of Ownership: EVs vs Petrol and Diesel Vehicles in India

## Understanding Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)

### Components of TCO

- Purchase price:
  - Ex-showroom, taxes, registration, and insurance.
- Running costs:
  - Fuel/charging cost per km.
- Maintenance and repairs:
  - Regular servicing and unscheduled fixes.
- Insurance:
  - Annual premiums, potentially higher IDV for EVs.
- Depreciation:
  - Resale value at the end of ownership period.

### Time horizon and usage

- TCO comparison:
  - Depends heavily on ownership duration (e.g., 5–8 years) and annual kilometers.
- Higher usage:
  - Magnifies differences in running cost; lower usage emphasizes upfront price.

---

## Purchase Price Differences

### EVs vs ICE vehicles

- EVs:
  - Typically higher upfront price due to battery cost.
- ICE (petrol/diesel):
  - Often cheaper to purchase for similar segment and equipment levels.

### Role of subsidies and tax benefits

- Central and state:
  - Incentives can reduce effective cost for qualifying EV models.
- Exemptions:
  - Road tax and registration fee waivers in some states.
- Changing policies:
  - Buyers must verify current schemes at purchase time.

---

## Fuel vs Electricity Cost

### Cost per km

- Petrol/diesel:
  - Fuel cost per km depends on:
    - Vehicle efficiency (km/l).
    - Fuel price per litre.
- EVs:
  - Electricity cost per km depends on:
    - Consumption (km/kWh).
    - Tariff per kWh.
    - Share of home/work AC charging vs expensive public DC charging.

### Typical patterns

- With home charging:
  - EV cost per km is often significantly lower than petrol/diesel equivalents.
- Using public DC frequently:
  - Narrows the cost advantage due to higher tariffs.

---

## Maintenance and Service Costs

### ICE vehicles

- Regular servicing:
  - Engine oil, filters, spark plugs, timing belts, exhaust components.
- Wear and tear:
  - More moving parts and complex systems that can fail.
- Emissions systems:
  - Turbochargers, catalytic converters, DPF in diesels.

### EVs

- Fewer moving parts:
  - No engine oil, fewer fluids, no exhaust system.
- Routine checks:
  - Brake fluid, coolant for thermal management (where applicable), cabin filter, tyres.
- Brake wear:
  - Lower due to regenerative braking doing much of the deceleration work.

---

## Insurance Considerations

### Premium differences

- EVs:
  - May have higher IDV and part costs; premiums can be somewhat higher.
- ICE:
  - Premiums may be lower initially but still influenced by segment, safety features, and claim history.

### Long-term impact

- Total insurance cost:
  - Over several years is one component of TCO but often overshadowed by fuel/charging and depreciation.

---

## Depreciation and Resale Value

### Current situation

- ICE vehicles:
  - Have well-understood resale patterns.
- EVs:
  - Resale market is still developing.
- Concerns:
  - Battery life, replacement cost, and rapid tech evolution.

### Factors influencing EV resale

- Brand strength:
  - Established brands with robust service networks retain value better.
- Model popularity:
  - Widely adopted models with known reliability are easier to resell.
- Battery health:
  - Documented good SoH supports better resale prices.

---

## How Usage Pattern Changes TCO Comparison

### Low annual kilometers

- Example:
  - Under 8,000–10,000 km/year.
- EV advantage:
  - Running cost difference may not fully offset higher purchase price within a short period.
- ICE:
  - May remain more cost-effective in pure monetary terms over short horizons.

### Moderate to high annual kilometers

- Example:
  - 15,000–25,000+ km/year.
- EV advantage:
  - Significant fuel savings can compensate higher upfront costs within a few years.
- Especially true:
  - When home AC charging is the main energy source.

---

## Segment-Specific TCO Dynamics

### Small hatchbacks and entry segment

- ICE:
  - Very low purchase price and reasonable fuel efficiency.
- EV:
  - Higher price but low running cost.
- TCO:
  - Depends heavily on usage; EV may win at higher annual kilometers.

### Compact SUVs and crossovers

- ICE:
  - Popular with moderate purchase and fuel costs.
- EV:
  - Higher price but strong city efficiency and lower maintenance.
- TCO:
  - Favourable for EVs at moderate to high usage, especially in urban environments.

### Premium and performance segments

- EVs:
  - Deliver strong performance with relatively lower running costs.
- ICE:
  - High-performance petrol/diesel vehicles can be expensive to fuel and maintain.
- TCO:
  - EV advantage increases for users who drive their premium vehicles frequently.

---

## Practical Approach to Comparing TCO

### Step 1: Estimate your usage

- Annual kilometers:
  - Based on commute, weekend trips, and occasional long journeys.
- Ownership duration:
  - How long you plan to keep the vehicle.

### Step 2: Calculate running costs

- For ICE:
  - (Annual km / km per litre) × fuel price per litre.
- For EV:
  - (Annual km / km per kWh) × electricity tariff per kWh.
  - Adjust for the proportion of home vs public charging.

### Step 3: Add other components

- Approximate:
  - Service costs per year for each option.
  - Insurance differences.
  - Expected depreciation (rough estimate).

---

## When an EV Makes Financial Sense

### Favourable conditions

- High annual kilometers.
- Reliable home/work AC charging.
- Access to any applicable subsidies or exemptions.
- Segment where EV pricing is competitive relative to feature set.

### Situations needing more caution

- No feasible home charging.
- Very low annual usage.
- Regions with limited public infrastructure and high dependence on long highway trips.

---

## Non-Financial Factors

### Driving experience

- EVs:
  - Smooth, quiet, instant torque, fewer vibrations.
- ICE:
  - Familiar refuelling, often wider service coverage in remote areas.

### Environmental and regulatory considerations

- Emissions:
  - EVs have no tailpipe emissions.
- Policy direction:
  - Increasing emphasis on electrification may influence future incentives and restrictions.

---

## Summary Checklist for TCO Comparison

- Define:
  - Annual km and planned years of ownership.
- Calculate:
  - Fuel vs electricity cost over that period.
- Add:
  - Maintenance and insurance estimates.
- Consider:
  - Resale and non-financial factors like comfort, noise, and convenience.
- Choose:
  - The option where both financial and practical aspects align best with your life.
