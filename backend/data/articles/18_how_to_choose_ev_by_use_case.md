# How to Choose an EV by Use Case in India

## Student Commute

### Typical needs

- Daily:
  - Short to moderate trips to college and tuition.
- Priorities:
  - Low running cost, easy parking, and simple charging.

### Suitable EV types

- Electric two-wheelers:
  - For short urban commutes with home charging.
- Small electric cars:
  - If budget allows and parking is available at home.

---

## Office Commute

### Daily patterns

- Predictable routes:
  - Regular travel to and from workplace.
- Distance:
  - Often within a city or between city and suburb.

### Suitable EV choices

- Two-wheelers:
  - For shorter commutes and single riders.
- Compact EV cars:
  - For comfort, safety, and occasional family use.
- Key factors:
  - Home or workplace charging access and realistic range sufficient for daily use.

---

## Family Car

### Usage profile

- Mixed:
  - School runs, shopping, office commute, and weekend outings.
- Priorities:
  - Space, comfort, safety, and low running cost.

### Ideal EV characteristics

- Body type:
  - Compact SUV or spacious hatchback/sedan.
- Range:
  - Enough for typical weekly patterns and occasional trips with minimal planning.
- Charging:
  - Reliable home charging; public chargers used infrequently.

---

## Delivery and Commercial Use

### High-utilization patterns

- Frequent stops:
  - Multiple deliveries per day.
- Distance:
  - Potentially high daily kilometers.

### Suitable EVs

- Two-wheelers:
  - For urban food and parcel delivery.
- Three-wheelers or small commercial EVs:
  - For heavier loads and structured routes.
- Consider:
  - Battery size, charging opportunities, and downtime for charging.

---

## Intercity Travel

### Use profile

- Long trips:
  - 200–400 km or more in a single day.
- Frequency:
  - Occasional or regular.

### EV considerations

- Range:
  - Models with higher real-world range and strong fast-charging support.
- Charging network:
  - Adequate CCS2 chargers along usual corridors.
- Backup:
  - Access to an alternate ICE vehicle or rental when infrastructure is sparse.

---

## Fleet Operation

### Operational characteristics

- Multiple vehicles:
  - Coordinated routes and schedules.
- Priorities:
  - Running cost, uptime, and predictability.

### Fleet EV types

- Electric three-wheelers:
  - For passenger or cargo use in defined areas.
- Commercial EV vans and trucks:
  - For urban distribution and hub-and-spoke systems.
- Infrastructure:
  - Central depot charging and maintenance facilities are key.

---

## Commercial Cargo Needs

### Cargo use cases

- Urban logistics:
  - Short to medium deliveries within or between city zones.
- Regional distribution:
  - Heavier loads over moderate distances.

### Choosing the right EV

- Payload:
  - Must meet cargo requirements after accounting for battery weight.
- Range:
  - Enough to cover typical routes with buffer.
- Charging:
  - Scheduled depot charging and possible on-route top-ups.

---

## Cross-Cutting Factors

### Budget and segment

- Budget:
  - Determines whether a two-wheeler, small car, or larger EV is feasible.
- Segment:
  - Choose segment based on passenger/cargo capacity and comfort needs.

### Daily kilometers and charging access

- Higher daily km:
  - Favour larger batteries and strong charging infrastructure.
- Limited charging:
  - May require careful planning or reconsideration of EV suitability for some use cases.

---

## Quick Match Guide

- Student:
  - Electric scooter/motorcycle or small car with home charging.
- Office worker:
  - Compact EV with sufficient range and home/work charging.
- Family:
  - Spacious EV with good safety and range, home charging essential.
- Delivery:
  - EV two-wheelers or three-wheelers with appropriate range and charging/swapping.
- Intercity:
  - Long-range EV cars with fast-charging support, or retain backup ICE access.
- Fleet:
  - Electric three-wheelers, vans, or trucks integrated into well-planned routes and depot charging.
