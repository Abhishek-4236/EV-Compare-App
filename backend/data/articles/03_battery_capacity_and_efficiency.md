# EV Battery Capacity and Efficiency

## Understanding Battery Capacity

### What kWh means

- EV battery capacity is measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh).
- One kWh represents:
  - The energy required to deliver 1 kW of power for 1 hour.
- Example:
  - A 40 kWh battery can theoretically deliver 10 kW of power for 4 hours, or 20 kW for 2 hours, under ideal conditions.

### Gross vs usable capacity

- Gross capacity:
  - The total physical capacity of the battery pack.
- Usable capacity:
  - The portion of the battery that the vehicle actually allows you to use.
- Buffering:
  - EVs keep a top and bottom buffer (e.g., 5–10% each side) to protect battery health and extend life.
- Practical implication:
  - A “40 kWh” pack might only offer, for example, 36 kWh usable, affecting real range.

## Energy Efficiency: km per kWh

### Why efficiency matters

- Range is not determined solely by battery size; it also depends on how efficiently the vehicle uses energy.
- Efficiency is often expressed as:
  - km/kWh (distance per unit energy), or
  - Wh/km (energy per unit distance).
- Higher km/kWh (or lower Wh/km) means:
  - More distance from the same battery capacity.

### Factors influencing efficiency

- Motor and inverter efficiency:
  - Better-designed drive units waste less energy as heat.
- Rolling resistance:
  - Low-resistance tyres and correct tyre pressures improve efficiency.
- Driving style:
  - Smooth, anticipatory driving with gentle acceleration is more efficient than aggressive throttle use.

## Why kWh Alone Does Not Determine Practicality

### Vehicle weight and size

- Heavier vehicles:
  - Need more energy to move, especially in stop-go traffic and on inclines.
- Example:
  - A 40 kWh battery in a heavy SUV may give similar range as a 30 kWh battery in a small, light hatchback.
- Larger wheels and tyres:
  - Can increase rolling resistance and reduce efficiency.

### Aerodynamics and shape

- Aerodynamic drag increases rapidly with speed.
- Taller, boxier vehicles:
  - Typically consume more energy at highway speeds than lower, sleeker vehicles.
- At city speeds:
  - Aerodynamics matter less; at 80–100 km/h, they matter a lot more.

### Intended use and environment

- City-focused EVs:
  - Often optimized for low-speed efficiency, quick acceleration, and compact size.
- Highway/cruising EVs:
  - May be tuned for stability and comfort at higher speeds; their efficiency depends strongly on driving speed.
- Climate:
  - Hot weather (AC use) and cold weather (heating and battery performance) change effective range.

## Examples of How Efficiency Changes Outcomes

### Example 1: Two cars, same battery, different efficiency

- Car A:
  - 40 kWh usable battery.
  - Efficiency: 7 km/kWh in mixed conditions.
  - Approximate range: 280 km.
- Car B:
  - 40 kWh usable battery.
  - Efficiency: 5 km/kWh in similar conditions.
  - Approximate range: 200 km.
- Lesson:
  - Same kWh, very different real-world practicality.

### Example 2: Smaller battery, better design

- Car C:
  - 30 kWh usable battery.
  - Efficiency: 7.5 km/kWh (light, aerodynamic hatchback).
  - Approximate range: 225 km.
- Car D:
  - 40 kWh usable battery.
  - Efficiency: 5 km/kWh (heavy SUV).
  - Approximate range: 200 km.
- Lesson:
  - The smaller-battery car can actually go further due to superior efficiency.

## Usable Capacity, SOC Window, and Daily Driving

### Why you rarely use 0–100%

- Batteries last longer if you:
  - Avoid deep discharges and long periods at 100% SOC.
- Many owners:
  - Typically use a comfortable window like 20–80% or 10–90%.
- Practical usable range:
  - Daily usable range is effectively the battery capacity within your preferred SOC window multiplied by efficiency.

### Planning with usable range

- If:
  - You treat 20–80% as your regular window (60% of usable capacity),
  - And your usable pack is 40 kWh with 7 km/kWh efficiency,
- Then:
  - Daily practical range without going near extremes ≈ 0.6 × 40 × 7 = 168 km.
- This is often more relevant than the theoretical maximum you could squeeze out from 0–100%.

## Role of Driving Conditions and Speed

### City vs highway

- City:
  - Frequent stops allow regenerative braking to work more, improving efficiency.
  - Very heavy congestion with AC can still reduce overall km/kWh.
- Highway:
  - Smooth speeds but higher aerodynamic drag.
  - Aggressive cruising at 110–120 km/h significantly cuts range compared to 80–90 km/h.

### Terrain and gradients

- Climbing:
  - Consumes more energy; heavy vehicles suffer more.
- Descending:
  - Regenerative braking partially recovers energy, but not all.
- Flat routes:
  - Are ideal for consistent and predictable efficiency.

## How Buyers Should Evaluate Battery Capacity

### Look beyond the kWh figure

- When comparing EVs:
  - Note both usable capacity and real-world efficiency.
- Ask or research:
  - Owner-reported km/kWh in conditions similar to your usual environment.
  - Independent tests or reviews that show actual highway and city consumption.

### Match capacity and efficiency to your use case

- Mostly city driving, moderate distances:
  - A smaller, efficient battery may be more cost-effective and lighter.
- Long commutes or regular intercity trips:
  - A larger battery with decent efficiency provides more buffer and reduces charging frequency.
- Limited charging options:
  - Higher usable capacity gives greater flexibility if you cannot charge every day.

### Consider cost versus benefit

- Larger batteries:
  - Increase upfront cost, vehicle weight, and sometimes insurance.
- If your daily usage is low:
  - Paying more for a very large pack that you rarely exploit may not be the best value.
- Focus on:
  - A balance between capacity, efficiency, and your actual needs.

---

## Checklist for Evaluating an EV’s Practical Battery Setup

### Before shortlisting

- Determine:
  - Your typical daily and weekly kilometers.
  - How often you can or want to charge (daily, alternate days, etc.).
- Define:
  - Minimum practical range you need with some buffer.

### When comparing models

- For each vehicle:
  - Check stated battery capacity and whether “usable” capacity is disclosed.
  - Look for km/kWh figures from real owners, not just claimed range.
- Assess:
  - Weight and body style implications on efficiency.

### After test driving

- Observe:
  - Consumption readings over a realistic route with AC on.
- Estimate:
  - If the effective daily range within a comfortable SOC window covers your patterns.
- Decide:
  - If the combination of battery size and efficiency fits your life better than just chasing the largest kWh number.
