# Claimed EV Range vs Real-World Range in India

## Why Claimed and Real-World Range Differ

### Standardized tests vs everyday driving

- Claimed EV range is usually measured using standardized test cycles under controlled conditions.
- These cycles assume:
  - Gentle acceleration and deceleration.
  - Limited use of HVAC (AC/heater).
  - Moderate, steady speeds.
  - Ideal tyre pressures and low accessory load.
- Real-world conditions in India are far more varied:
  - Stop-go traffic.
  - Mixed city and highway driving.
  - High ambient temperatures.
  - Varying road quality and gradients.

### Test cycles and their limitations

- Test cycles are designed to allow comparison between vehicles, not to predict your exact daily result.
- They often:
  - Do not include heavy congestion or long idling with AC.
  - Under‑represent steep gradients, flyovers, and hilly terrain.
  - Use moderate payload (not fully loaded with passengers and luggage).
- As a result, real-world drivers typically see lower range than the brochure claim.

## Key Factors Affecting Real-World Range

### Driving and riding speed

- Higher speeds increase aerodynamic drag exponentially.
- On highways:
  - Cruising at 100–120 km/h can significantly reduce range compared to 70–80 km/h.
- In the city:
  - Smooth, anticipatory driving with gentle acceleration helps efficiency.
  - Aggressive throttle inputs and frequent hard braking waste energy that could otherwise be recovered.

### Terrain and road conditions

- Climbing:
  - Uphill stretches, flyovers, and ghat sections increase energy consumption.
- Descending:
  - Regenerative braking can recover some energy, but never 100%.
- Poor roads:
  - Frequent braking, steering corrections, and speed changes lower efficiency.

### Payload and passengers

- Heavier vehicles require more energy to move.
- Full cabin:
  - Four or five adults plus luggage will consume more energy than a single driver.
- Roof carriers or rear racks:
  - Add both weight and aerodynamic drag.

### Weather and climate

- Heat:
  - High temperatures common in India increase AC use, which draws significant power.
- Cold:
  - In colder regions, battery chemistry becomes less efficient and cabin heating consumes energy.
- Humidity:
  - Increases AC compressor load due to dehumidification needs.

### AC usage and in-cabin comfort

- Air conditioning:
  - Continuous use at low temperature and high fan speed reduces range.
- Cabin pre‑conditioning:
  - If done while charging, can shift some energy load to the grid instead of the battery.
- Seat and steering heaters (where available):
  - Typically more efficient than heating the entire cabin but still affect range.

### Traffic and driving pattern

- Stop-go city traffic:
  - Can be efficient if you use regenerative braking effectively.
  - Becomes inefficient if you have prolonged crawling with AC on and little opportunity for regen.
- Long idling with AC:
  - EVs do not consume energy “idling” like ICE engines, but HVAC and accessories still draw power.

### Battery state of charge (SoC) and management

- Batteries are most efficient in the mid range of SoC.
- Near full:
  - Regeneration can be limited to protect the battery.
- Near empty:
  - Some vehicles reduce power to protect the pack, altering driving pattern.
- Thermal management:
  - Energy is used to keep the battery in a safe temperature window.

## How Real-World Range Varies in India

### City-only usage

- Typical scenario:
  - Moderate speeds, plenty of regenerative braking, heavy AC usage in summer.
- Outcome:
  - Real-world range can be close to, or somewhat below, claimed if driving is smooth.
  - Heavy traffic with constant crawling and AC can bring it down further.

### Mixed city and highway

- Common pattern:
  - 60–80% city, 20–40% ring road or highway.
- Outcome:
  - Often 60–80% of claimed range depending on speeds and driving style.
  - Speed discipline on highways (80–90 km/h) helps maintain better range.

### Highway-focused usage

- Typical:
  - Long stretches at 90–120 km/h with fewer stops.
- Outcome:
  - Range often drops to 50–70% of claimed, especially at higher cruising speeds.
  - Use of cruise control can help slightly if speeds are reasonable.

## Interpreting Range Claims When Comparing EVs

### Treat claimed range as an upper bound

- Use brochure figures only as a relative comparison between models, not as a guaranteed number.
- For planning:
  - Assume you will realistically get around 60–70% of the claimed figure in mixed Indian conditions.

### Look for efficiency, not just battery size

- Two EVs with the same battery capacity can have very different ranges due to:
  - Motor efficiency.
  - Vehicle weight.
  - Aerodynamics.
  - Tyre type and size.
- Check:
  - User reports and independent tests for real-world consumption (Wh/km or km/kWh).

### Consider your worst-case scenario

- Identify your most demanding regular use case:
  - Example: 90 km daily commute with AC always on, or 250 km weekend highway trip.
- Choose an EV that:
  - Covers this use case with at least 20–30% buffer on real-world range.
  - Does not depend on a mid-journey charge under normal circumstances.

### Use range buffers and planning

- Daily use:
  - Aim to finish the day with some SoC left rather than returning with near zero.
- Long trips:
  - Plan around fast chargers with realistic spacing, not brochure range.
  - Account for traffic delays, detours, and weather.

## Practical Buyer Checklist for Range

### Before shortlisting

- Decide:
  - Your minimum acceptable real-world range based on daily and weekly patterns.
- Research:
  - Real-world owner feedback from similar climates and cities.

### When comparing models

- For each EV:
  - Note claimed range.
  - Estimate realistic range as 60–70% of claimed for mixed use.
  - Check battery capacity and reported efficiency (km/kWh).
- Prefer:
  - EVs that offer good efficiency rather than only large batteries.

### At the test-drive stage

- During test drive:
  - Keep AC on at normal settings.
  - Observe energy consumption and how quickly predicted range drops versus distance travelled.
- Ask:
  - Sales staff to show different drive modes and their impact on range.

---

## Summary: Using Range Claims Sensibly

- Claimed range is a standardized comparison tool, not a promise.
- Real-world range in India depends on:
  - Speed, traffic, terrain, payload, weather, HVAC usage, and driving style.
- For most buyers:
  - Planning with a 30–40% discount on claimed range gives a safer picture.
- When choosing an EV:
  - Focus on whether the realistic range comfortably supports your real life, not just the biggest number on the brochure.
