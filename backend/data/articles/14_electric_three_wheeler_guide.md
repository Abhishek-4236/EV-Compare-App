# Electric Three-Wheeler Guide for India

## Use Cases: Passenger and Cargo

### Passenger three-wheelers

- Common use:
  - Last-mile urban and semi-urban transport.
- Priorities:
  - Uptime, comfort, and low per-km operating cost.
- Passenger safety:
  - Stable design and adequate braking are important.

### Cargo three-wheelers

- Applications:
  - E-commerce deliveries, local goods transport, and intra-city logistics.
- Priorities:
  - Payload capacity, loading convenience, and route suitability.
- Vehicle type:
  - Open or closed cargo boxes depending on goods and weather protection needs.

---

## Daily Duty Cycle and Route Planning

### Typical daily operations

- Fixed routes:
  - Known start and end points with predictable distance.
- Variable routes:
  - Dependent on daily demand; more challenging for planning.

### Matching range to duty cycle

- Determine:
  - Daily kilometers including any detours.
- Plan:
  - Charging times (midday breaks or overnight) to match required range.
- Buffer:
  - Keep margin for unexpected trips or traffic delays.

---

## Charging Needs and Infrastructure

### Depot and home charging

- For small fleets:
  - Charging at a company depot or driver’s home is common.
- Requirement:
  - Sufficient AC charging points and electrical capacity.

### Public charging for three-wheelers

- Some cities:
  - Offer charging hubs tailored to commercial three-wheelers.
- Availability:
  - Must be checked carefully; not all public chargers suit three-wheeler connectors and power needs.

---

## Payload and Vehicle Specifications

### Payload capacity

- Check:
  - Rated payload and how it affects performance and range.
- Overloading:
  - Increases energy consumption and can impact reliability and safety.

### Motor and battery configuration

- Motor:
  - Power and torque should meet your load and terrain requirements.
- Battery:
  - Capacity chosen based on daily distance and charging opportunities.
- Chemistry:
  - LFP or other chemistries may be used, with impacts on cycle life and performance.

---

## Route Suitability

### Terrain and road quality

- Urban:
  - Stop-go traffic with frequent start/stop cycles benefits from regen.
- Hilly routes:
  - Require stronger motors and adequate braking/regen capacity.
- Poor roads:
  - Good suspension and robust construction are essential.

### Weather conditions

- Monsoon:
  - Protection for passengers or cargo and electrical safety.
- Heat:
  - Adequate cooling for battery and power electronics.

---

## Fleet Economics and Operating Cost

### Running cost calculations

- Electricity vs fuel:
  - EV three-wheelers generally have lower running cost per km than CNG/diesel.
- Maintenance:
  - Lower for EV drivelines; still need regular checks on tyres, brakes, and suspension.
- Downtime:
  - Charging time must be factored into operational planning.

### Revenue and utilization

- Higher uptime:
  - Improves revenue potential for operators.
- Efficient route planning:
  - Balances battery use and charging time to maximize daily utilization.

---

## Maintenance and Service Support

### Regular maintenance needs

- Focus:
  - Tyres, brakes, suspension, steering components, and electrical connections.
- Battery:
  - Health checks and software diagnostics where supported.

### Service network

- Choose:
  - Brands with established support in your region.
- Parts:
  - Availability of common wear items and critical electrical components.

---

## Comparison Factors Before Purchase

### Technical and operational factors

- Range:
  - Suitable for your duty cycle with buffer.
- Payload:
  - Matches your typical load profile.
- Charging:
  - Feasible depot/home charging and any public backup options.

### Financial and support factors

- Upfront cost:
  - Including charger infrastructure if needed.
- Running cost:
  - Estimated per km.
- Warranty:
  - Battery, motor, and controller coverage.
- Service:
  - Proximity and responsiveness of support.

---

## Suitability Summary

- Electric three-wheelers:
  - Fit well where routes and duty cycles are predictable and infrastructure is manageable.
- Operators:
  - Benefit most when they carefully match vehicle specs to payload, terrain, and daily distance.
- Decision:
  - Must balance purchase cost, operating economics, and support to ensure sustainable operations.
